== 2. Random split thresholds on QUANT's features (idea 17: rank-uniform splits) ==
share = expected share of training series on the smaller side of a root split, threshold uniform in [min, max]
(scikit-learn's ExtraTrees); a threshold uniform over the ranks would give about 0.25 for every feature
  raw               : median share 0.141; features with share < 0.10: 27.1% (mean over datasets)
  1st difference    : median share 0.124; features with share < 0.10: 36.5% (mean over datasets)
  2nd difference    : median share 0.116; features with share < 0.10: 43.1% (mean over datasets)
  Fourier magnitude : median share 0.131; features with share < 0.10: 30.9% (mean over datasets)
  all views         : features with share < 0.10: 34.8% (mean over datasets)
  does it line up with QUANT's losses? Spearman(unbalanced share, QUANT - MR-Hydra) = -0.09 (p = 0.3); many-class datasets 42% vs others 34%

== 3. Where the ensemble's gain and cost come from (idea 18: second opinion only when needed) ==
one core, resample 0: QUANT 15.7 min; Hydra adds 50.0 min (46.3 of it computing features)
test series are 67% of all series and take 59% of Hydra's feature time
  the 20 datasets with the largest gains: 66% of the total gain, 6% of Hydra's extra time (found with test results: an upper bound)
  the 40 datasets with the largest gains: 94% of the total gain, 13% of Hydra's extra time (found with test results: an upper bound)
Simple rules that use only the training data's size (QUANT accuracy 10 resamples: 85.36%, full ensemble 87.25%):
  Hydra only if classes >= 10                     :  32 datasets, accuracy 86.40%, keeps 55% of the gain, total 24.6 min on one core
  Hydra only if <= 10 training series per class   :  33 datasets, accuracy 86.27%, keeps 48% of the gain, total 21.0 min on one core
  Hydra only if classes >= 10 or <= 10 per class  :  53 datasets, accuracy 86.62%, keeps 66% of the gain, total 27.5 min on one core
